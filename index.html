How this compass works, and where the words come from.

這是啥物？What is this?

形數羅盤 teaches geometry and basic math words in four ways at once: Tâi-gí (Taiwanese Hokkien, written in POJ romanization), 漢羅 Han characters, 華文 Mandarin, and English. It's built for children — and anyone — curious about doing math in Taiwanese.

按怎耍 How to use it

1. On the left, the round compass has five wedges: 形狀 Shapes, 角 Angles, 線 Lines, 數字 Numbers, and 符號 Symbols. Tap one to load it.
2. In the Learn tab, tap any card to flip it and reveal the word in all four languages, plus a kid-friendly note. Tap ⭐ to save words you've mastered.
3. In the Match Game tab, tap a shape tile, then tap the Taigi name that goes with it. Matched pairs turn green.
4. Your starred words are tracked in the progress bar on the left, and can be exported any time.

羅馬字小提醒 About the romanization

Taigi words are written in POJ (白話字), e.g. saⁿ-kak-hêng for 三角形 "triangle." Tone marks sit above vowels — don't worry about reading them perfectly at first; just say the word out loud and compare it with the Mandarin and English.

詞源與字義故事 Source & Etymology

Vocabulary adapted from a community-maintained Taigi math-terms glossary (tutumasa.blogspot.com, "一寡數學 ê 台語詞"), cross-checked against Taiwanese Hokkien dictionaries. The names are often descriptive and visual:

  • 鴨卵形 (ah-nn̄g-hêng) / 雞卵形 (ke-nn̄g-hêng): Literally "duck-egg shape" and "chicken-egg shape" — these are the traditional Taiwanese terms for an oval or ellipse.
  • 橄欖形 (kaⁿ-ná-hêng): Literally "olive shape" — colloquial term for a rhombus or diamond.
  • 長篙形 (tn̂g-ko-hêng): Literally "long bamboo pole shape" — another colloquial name for a rectangle.
  • 斜糕形 (chhiâ-ko-hêng): "Slanted/tilted traditional cake shape" — historically used for a rhombus or trapezoid.
  • 斜拄形 (chhiâ-tu-hêng): "Slanted/tilted pillar shape" — the Taiwanese term for a trapezoid.
  • 屈尺角 (khut-chhioh-kak): "L-shaped carpenter's square ruler corner" — refers to a right angle (90°).
  • 斜角逝 (chho̍ah-kak-chōa) / 坦斜逝 (thán-chho̍ah-chōa): "Slanted/tilted line" — colloquial terms for a diagonal line (where 逝 chōa/tsuā means a line or path in Taigi).
  • 齊頭 (chê-thâu) / 齊頭數 (chê-thâu-siàu): "Aligned heads" — refers to neat, whole numbers or integers.
